# 4D Framework (AI Fluency)

## What it is
The four core competencies of [[concepts/ai-fluency]], developed by Dakan & Feller. Every effective human-AI interaction exercises some combination of these four dimensions:

| Dimension | What it means |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Delegation** | Deciding which tasks go to AI vs. staying with the human |
| **Description** | Communicating clearly with AI — the craft of the prompt |
| **Discernment** | Critically evaluating AI output before acting on it |
| **Diligence** | Using AI responsibly; maintaining accountability for outcomes |

## Why it matters (for this domain)
The 4Ds give a vocabulary for diagnosing AI collaboration failures. Most problems fall into one of four buckets: wrong task assigned to AI (Delegation), poorly specified request (Description), output not scrutinised (Discernment), or accountability not maintained (Diligence). Naming the failure type is the first step to fixing it.

## How it works
The 4Ds are not a sequence — they're simultaneous lenses. Before sending a prompt: is this the right task for AI? (Delegation). While writing it: is my request clear? (Description). After receiving output: is this actually correct? (Discernment). Throughout: am I responsible for this output? (Diligence).

## Practical notes
- **Delegation** is underrated — choosing *not* to use AI is a skill. Over-delegation leads to loss of judgment; under-delegation leaves productivity on the table.
- **Description** is what most people call "prompt engineering" — but framing it as communication is more useful than framing it as engineering.
- **Discernment** is most critical on high-stakes outputs: code going to production, analysis going to stakeholders, advice given to clients.
- **Diligence** is the ethical dimension — AI makes it easy to produce content at scale; Diligence asks who is accountable for it.
